## Definition

**Anankastický režim** (anankastic regime) is a state of extreme cognitive rigidity and thought closure, characterized by systematic rejection of belief revision and gradual epistemic isolation. Drawing its name from the Greek *ananke* (necessity, compulsion) — the same root as the clinical term *anankastic personality* (compulsive-rigid personality structure) — the term in this taxonomy denotes a **systemic state**, not a personality trait. It can emerge in any subject under sustained exposure to certain epistemic anihilation techniques.

Phenomenologically, the anankastic regime presents as:

- **Compulsive defense** of held beliefs against any contradicting evidence
- **Hyperbolic certainty** disproportionate to actual evidential support
- **Diminishing tolerance** for epistemic ambiguity, suspended judgment, or productive doubt
- **Recursive justification loops** that amplify rather than examine
- **Progressive social isolation** from sources offering alternative perspectives
- **Affective hyperreactivity** when held views are questioned

Structurally, the regime represents an **epistemic phase transition** — the system flips from healthy, revisable belief-holding to a self-stabilizing closed configuration. Once consolidated, the regime resists standard corrective mechanisms: argument, evidence, and social feedback all lose effectiveness because they are filtered through the very rigidity they would need to penetrate.

## Context

The anankastic regime functions in the [Kompendium](/kompendium/) as both a **terminal state** of certain epistemic annihilation trajectories and a **prerequisite** for the deepest forms of cognitive annihilation. Several techniques in the taxonomy are documented as inducers:

- Prolonged exposure to **EA-1.1.1 Selective Exposure** in conjunction with social isolation
- Sustained **EA-1.3.1 Immunization Against Falsification** training in dogmatic communities
- The **"Guru and Adept" pattern** when consolidation phase reaches recursive validation loops
- Combinations of **EA-1.4.2 Latent Emotional Modulation** with persistent identity-threat framing

Clinically, the regime resembles certain dimensions of **delusional disorder**, but with two distinguishing features: (1) the rigidity is **content-general** rather than focused on specific delusional themes; (2) it can typically be traced to **environmental induction** rather than primarily endogenous etiology.

In Prismatic-relevant defensive applications, the regime serves as a **negative outcome marker** for epistemic security operations. The [Decision Engine](/hub/dd/decisions/) calibration component treats indicators of anankastic-regime-style reasoning in source materials as strong negative signals: a source displaying compulsive rigidity is unlikely to reliably revise in response to new evidence, regardless of its formal credentials.

Recovery from the anankastic regime is possible but typically requires **extended structural intervention** — sustained exposure to alternative epistemic communities, supervised metacognitive rehabilitation, and gradual reconstruction of epistemic flexibility. Pharmacological intervention alone is rarely sufficient.
