## Definition

**Coercive control** is a sociological and clinical framework developed by Evan Stark (*Coercive Control: How Men Entrap Women in Personal Life*, 2007) reconceptualizing intimate partner abuse not as a sequence of discrete violent incidents but as an **ongoing strategic pattern of domination**. The framework identifies four operational vectors: **violence or its credible threat**, **intimidation** (degradation, surveillance, threats to children or pets), **isolation** (severing victim's contact with family, friends, employment, financial independence), and **control** (micro-regulation of daily life — what to wear, eat, say, when to sleep, how to spend money).

Stark's central theoretical move is to relocate the harm from the body to the **liberty interest**: coercive control is a "liberty crime" producing what he terms a *condition of unfreedom*. The pattern's hallmark is its **diffuse and cumulative** character — individual acts may appear trivial in isolation (a text message asking "where are you?", a comment about an outfit) but constitute a totalizing regime when integrated over time.

Coercive control was criminalized in England and Wales in 2015 (Serious Crime Act §76), Scotland in 2018, Ireland in 2019, and in several US states. The legal innovation lies in recognizing **course-of-conduct offences** that aggregate non-violent acts into a prosecutable pattern. Empirically, coercive control predicts intimate partner homicide more strongly than prior physical violence — a finding now central to risk assessment instruments such as DASH and the Stalking Risk Profile.

## Context

Within the Prismatic Platform, coercive control serves as a **structural analogue** to systematic epistemic annihilation operating at the dyadic relational scale rather than the group or societal scale.

- The Kompendium's [/kompendium/06-klinicke-aplikace/](@/kompendium/06-klinicke-aplikace/_index.md) chapter explicitly maps coercive control patterns onto EA technique clusters: isolation corresponds to EA-1.1.1 (selective exposure), intimidation involves EA-1.3.1 (immunization through fear), micro-regulation enacts EA-2 (cognitive annihilation via attention capture).
- The [Anankastický režim](@/glossary/anankasticky-rezim.md) concept extends coercive control's "regime" framing into the epistemic domain — both share the logic of ambient, totalizing, low-intensity domination.
- The {{ <cross_link path="/hub/dd/decisions/" text="Decision Engine" /> }} flags information sourced from individuals reporting coercive-control patterns as requiring elevated provenance scrutiny — testimony emerging from such relationships systematically underreports severity due to victim accommodation.
- [DARVO](@/glossary/darvo.md) is one of the most reliable verbal signatures of coercive controllers under accountability pressure.
- [Color Teams](@/glossary/color-teams.md) Blue Team protocols include coercive-control screening when modeling threat actors operating through proxies (compromised family members, intimate partners).

Coercive control demonstrates that **systematic manipulation does not require formal organizational structure** — a single perpetrator can implement a totalist regime within a private dyad.
