## Definition

**DARVO** — an acronym for **Deny, Attack, Reverse Victim and Offender** — names a stereotyped three-phase response pattern that some perpetrators deploy when confronted with their misconduct. The construct was formalized by psychologist **Jennifer J. Freyd** (1997) in the context of betrayal-trauma research and has since been validated empirically across domains including sexual harassment, intimate partner abuse, institutional misconduct, and political accountability.

The three phases unfold sequentially or in rapid alternation:

1. **Deny** — flat rejection that the conduct occurred, that it was harmful, or that the accuser's interpretation is reasonable ("That never happened," "You're misremembering," "It wasn't like that").
2. **Attack** — assault on the credibility, motives, mental health, or character of the accuser ("She's unstable," "He's been planning this for months," "This is politically motivated").
3. **Reverse Victim and Offender (RVO)** — re-narration of the encounter such that the original perpetrator becomes the wronged party and the original victim becomes the aggressor ("I'm the one being harassed here," "This false accusation has destroyed my life").

Empirical studies (Harsey, Zurbriggen, Freyd 2017; Harsey & Freyd 2020) demonstrate that DARVO exposure causally **reduces observer belief in the accuser** and increases blame attribution to the victim — even among informed audiences. This makes DARVO not merely a defensive reflex but an **active epistemic weapon** that exploits third-party cognitive heuristics about confidence, distress, and narrative coherence.

DARVO is most reliably observed in perpetrators with high entitlement, low remorse, and prior practice — career manipulators, institutional defenders, and individuals with antisocial or narcissistic traits. Its presence is a strong (though not pathognomonic) signal of bad-faith engagement.

## Context

Within the Prismatic Platform Kompendium, DARVO is catalogued under **EA-1.3.2 (epistemic immunization through accuser-delegitimization)** — see [/kompendium/techniques/ea-1-3-2/](/kompendium/techniques/ea-1-3-2/). It is one of the most operationally important EA techniques because it weaponizes third-party epistemics: a successful DARVO does not need to convince the original victim, only to fracture the surrounding evidentiary community.

- The [Decision Engine](/hub/dd/decisions/) treats DARVO indicators as **negative source-credibility signals** when ingesting denials in due-diligence contexts. Witness testimony showing the three-phase signature is downweighted; original accusations are *not* upweighted (DARVO is not corroboration), but their downweighting is suspended.
- [Color Teams](@/glossary/color-teams.md) Red Team adversary models include DARVO scripts when simulating media-savvy threat actors and reputational-defense operations.
- The [Delegitimizace](@/glossary/delegitimizace.md) entry catalogues the broader family of accuser-delegitimization techniques of which DARVO is the most stereotyped instance.
- DARVO frequently co-occurs with [Gaslighting](@/glossary/gaslighting.md) (Phase 1: Deny) and [Coercive Control](@/glossary/coercive-control.md) signature patterns.

Recognition of DARVO is included in clinical training materials at [/kompendium/06-klinicke-aplikace/](@/kompendium/06-klinicke-aplikace/_index.md) and in OSINT investigative workflows for reputation-management forensics.
