## Definition

A **deepfake** is synthetic audiovisual content generated by deep neural networks (originally GAN-based, now predominantly diffusion-based and transformer-conditioned) that depicts an identifiable person saying or doing something they did not say or do. The term, coined on Reddit in 2017, has expanded from face-swap video to encompass voice cloning (≤3 seconds of reference audio suffices for many systems), full-body synthesis, real-time avatar puppeteering, and document forgery (e.g., synthetic passports, medical reports).

Technically, contemporary deepfakes exploit several factorizations: **identity disentanglement** (separating "who" from "what they're doing"), **temporal coherence priors** (suppressing flicker artifacts), and **alignment to biometric anchors** (lip-sync to phonemes, gaze to off-screen referents). Detection has become an arms race: classical artifacts (asymmetric blink rates, inconsistent corneal reflections, frequency-domain noise patterns) are progressively eliminated by each model generation.

Deepfakes constitute an **epistemic threat surface** distinct from textual disinformation because audiovisual content carries an evolved *prima facie* credibility — humans encode "seeing is believing" as a deep heuristic. Three secondary effects compound the primary forgery harm: (1) the **liar's dividend** — genuine recordings can now be plausibly denied as fakes; (2) the **chilling effect** on testimonial trust generally; (3) **non-consensual intimate imagery** weaponization, which constitutes the dominant harmful use-case in current empirical surveys (96%+ of in-the-wild deepfakes per Sensity 2019, 2023 follow-ups).

Provenance-based defenses (C2PA content credentials, hardware-anchored capture attestation, watermarking) are being deployed but face fundamental adversarial dynamics — any defense visible to the attacker can be optimized against.

## Context

Within the Prismatic Platform, deepfakes are modeled as a **first-class threat vector** in several subsystems:

- The {{ <cross_link path="/hub/dd/decisions/" text="Decision Engine" /> }} Source Assessment stage applies provenance-confidence scoring to ingested audiovisual evidence; unsigned media from low-trust channels are downweighted regardless of apparent content.
- [NABLA Infinity](@/glossary/nabla-infinity.md) implements multi-modal cross-checks: claims attested by single-channel evidence (video only, audio only) carry lower epistemic weight than multi-channel corroboration with independent provenance.
- [Color Teams](@/glossary/color-teams.md) Red Team scenarios include deepfake-enabled attack patterns: synthetic-CEO fraud, fabricated political endorsements, false-flag testimonial videos.
- The Kompendium catalogues deepfake-enabled techniques under **EA-1.2 (epistemic falsification)** with cross-references at [/kompendium/07-teoreticke-reflexe/](@/kompendium/07-teoreticke-reflexe/_index.md) regarding the philosophical implications for testimonial epistemology.
- [Hyperreality](@/glossary/hyperreality.md) — Baudrillard's concept — is increasingly literal under deepfake saturation: simulacra precede and replace the real.

Deepfakes are not merely a "fake content" problem; they are a **systemic erosion of audiovisual evidence** as a category, with downstream consequences for journalism, law, intelligence, and interpersonal trust.
