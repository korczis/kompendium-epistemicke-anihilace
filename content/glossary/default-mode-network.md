## Definition

The **Default Mode Network (DMN)** is a large-scale brain network — first systematically described by Raichle and colleagues (2001) — that exhibits coordinated activity during periods of internal cognition: mind-wandering, autobiographical recall, future simulation, theory-of-mind reasoning, and self-referential evaluation. Its principal nodes are the **medial prefrontal cortex (mPFC)**, **posterior cingulate cortex (PCC)**, **precuneus**, **angular gyrus**, and the **hippocampal formation**. The defining functional signature is **task-negative activation**: DMN nodes deactivate during externally-focused attention and re-engage when attention is released from the environment.

The DMN is now understood as the neural substrate of the **narrative self** — the integrated, autobiographical, temporally-extended sense of identity that humans construct from episodic memory, prospective imagination, and ongoing interpretive schemas. Damage to or atypical connectivity within the DMN is implicated in disorders of self-experience: depression (excessive self-referential rumination), Alzheimer's disease (DMN is among the earliest networks to show amyloid deposition), schizophrenia (boundary disturbance between self and non-self), depersonalization/derealization disorders, and autism spectrum conditions.

Pharmacological agents that disrupt DMN integrity — psilocybin, LSD, ketamine, sustained meditation — produce phenomenological reports of "ego dissolution" or "self-transcendence," providing convergent evidence that the experiential sense of being a unified self is in part a DMN-mediated construction. Sleep, anesthesia, and severe trauma also alter DMN connectivity in measurable ways.

## Context

In the Prismatic threat model, the DMN is a **high-value target** for the most severe class of epistemic annihilation techniques — those aiming not merely at specific beliefs but at the substrate of integrated identity itself. Kompendium techniques in the existential/identity-disruption family (EA-3.x) operate by introducing sustained pressure on DMN-supported processes: forced rumination on contradictory autobiographical narratives, induction of derealization through perceptual destabilization, exploitation of memory reconsolidation windows, and disruption of the temporal continuity that the DMN normally maintains.

Platform-level defenses include: (1) detecting interaction patterns suggestive of derealization or dissociative state in user inputs and routing those sessions toward de-escalation rather than further analytical engagement; (2) the Decision Engine treating user reports of identity disturbance as **categorical confidence-suspending signals**; (3) clinical-referral workflows in the DD/HR-screening surfaces when DMN-disruption indicators appear in subject behavioral histories.
