## Definition

**Delegitimizace** (*delegitimization*) is the systematic process by which persons, institutions, knowledge sources, or epistemic procedures are stripped of their **legitimate authority** to function as reliable inputs to belief formation. Unlike mere disagreement or critique — which target the *content* of claims — delegitimization targets the *standing* of the claimant. After successful delegitimization, the same true statement, made by the same competent source, no longer registers as evidence: the channel itself has been disabled at the structural level.

Within the [Kompendium epistemické a kognitivní anihilace](/kompendium/), delegitimization is treated as a **structural prerequisite** for advanced annihilation scenarios. Scénář F (institutional collapse), Scénář G (mass epistemic capture), and Scénář H (terminal alienation) all presuppose successful delegitimization of background authorities — without it, the targeted subject retains anchor points from which to reconstruct an adequate picture of reality.

Delegitimization operates at three nested levels: **personal** (specific individuals — therapists, journalists, family members — are reframed as unreliable, biased, or hostile), **institutional** (entire categories — academia, courts, regulatory bodies, independent media — are reframed as captured or corrupt), and **methodological** (entire epistemic procedures — peer review, statistical reasoning, expert testimony — are reframed as inherently invalid). Multi-level delegitimization is the most dangerous: it removes not only the trusted source but the very *type* of source the subject might turn to next.

## Context

Within the Prismatic Platform, delegitimization is tracked as a **distinct threat signal** separate from ordinary information conflict:

- The [Decision Engine](/hub/dd/decisions/) flags decisions whose information graph shows source-reputation collapse for previously high-confidence inputs — a pattern characteristic of active delegitimization rather than normal evidence revision.
- The [Color Teams](/glossary/color-teams/) Red Team simulates delegitimization campaigns against platform-internal authority structures (compliance officers, audit logs, calibration data) to verify that no single delegitimization vector can blind the operator.
- [NABLA Infinity](/glossary/nabla-infinity/) maintains **independent provenance ledgers** so that even if a particular source is delegitimized, the historical record of when it agreed with other sources remains queryable — preventing retroactive rewriting of trust relationships.
- Czech-language treatment of common patterns (preemptive delegitimization, ad-hominem cascades, "everyone is corrupt" sweeps) is at [/kompendium/03-taxonomie/delegitimizace/](/kompendium/03-taxonomie/delegitimizace/) and feeds directly into [taxonomie EA](/glossary/taxonomie-ea/) at codes EA-2.x.

Common patterns include **preemptive framing** (delegitimizing a likely future critic *before* they speak), **selective skepticism** (extreme epistemic standards applied only to disfavored sources), **whataboutism cascades** (any critique becomes evidence of the critic's bias), and **terminal moves** ("nobody can be trusted on this"). The terminal move is diagnostic: when a subject reports that *no* source remains trustworthy, [epistemická alienace](/glossary/epistemicka-alienace/) is typically advanced.

Delegitimization is **legitimate** when targeted authorities are in fact unreliable — the technique is dual-use. The diagnostic distinction is whether the delegitimization is **selective** (favoring a particular alternative narrative) or **principled** (applying consistent standards). Selective delegitimization that systematically clears the field for a single replacement narrative is the manipulative form.
