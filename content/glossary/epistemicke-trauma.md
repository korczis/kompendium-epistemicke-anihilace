## Definition

**Epistemické trauma** (*epistemic trauma*) is structural damage to the capacity to trust one's own cognition that persists after exposure to [epistemická anihilace](@/glossary/epistemicka-anihilace.md) or related sustained manipulation. Clinically it overlaps substantially with **PTSD** (post-traumatic stress disorder) and especially **cPTSD** (complex PTSD, developed under prolonged interpersonal violence), but the [Kompendium epistemické a kognitivní anihilace](@/kompendium/_index.md) maintains a distinct term because traditional trauma diagnostics target affective and behavioral dimensions and underspecify the epistemic ones.

Three structural features distinguish epistemic trauma from PTSD/cPTSD even where they co-occur:

1. **Disturbance of evidential givenness** — phenomena no longer present themselves *as themselves* with normal clarity. Routine perception, memory retrieval, and inference feel uncertain regardless of their factual reliability. (The phenomenological dimension; see [fenomenologie](@/glossary/fenomenologie.md).)
2. **Metacognitive miscalibration** — the subject's confidence reports systematically diverge from their accuracy, in either direction. Some subjects become pathologically over-confident (counterphobic compensation); more commonly, persistent global under-confidence persists even on tasks where calibration is objectively achievable.
3. **Source-trust collapse** — beyond traumatic relational distrust, a structural unwillingness to treat *any* source — including one's own first-person observations — as evidence. This is the diagnostic signature of advanced [epistemická alienace](@/glossary/epistemicka-alienace.md) and the most resistant clinical feature.

Recovery trajectories from epistemic trauma have been studied less systematically than from PTSD/cPTSD, but the kompendium synthesizes available data and clinical experience into a tentative staged model: **stabilization** (re-establishing baseline functioning and safety), **scaffolded re-trust** (graduated re-engagement with low-stakes evidence using explicit external scaffolding such as written records and trusted third parties), **calibration repair** (deliberate practice on tasks with objective feedback), and **integration** (re-incorporation of the EA experience into a coherent autobiographical narrative without re-traumatization).

## Context

Within the Prismatic Platform, the recovery model informs platform design as much as direct user-facing tooling:

- The {{ cross_link(path="/hub/dd/decisions/", text="Decision Engine") }} is structurally designed for **scaffolded re-trust**: explicit external records, calibration feedback, and source-diversity tracking are exactly the affordances epistemic-trauma recovery requires. A subject in recovery can rely on the platform's metacognitive scaffolding while their own [metakognice](@/glossary/metakognice.md) is rebuilt.
- [NABLA Infinity](@/glossary/nabla-infinity.md) maintains **immutable observation logs** that survive subjective doubt — a critical feature when the subject's own confidence in their memory has been damaged. The record exists independent of the subject's current epistemic state.
- The [Color Teams](@/glossary/color-teams.md) Blue Team includes scenarios drawn from documented epistemic-trauma recovery cases — verifying that platform UX does not inadvertently reproduce trauma-adjacent patterns (sudden silent record changes, unexplained reframings, suppressed user-confirmed events).
- The kompendium explicitly cautions against premature **content-level intervention**: arguing about the truth of specific claims with a subject whose epistemic-trust capacity is damaged often re-traumatizes rather than helps. Structural restoration must precede content restoration. Czech-language treatment: [/kompendium/05-recovery/trauma/](@/kompendium/recovery-protocols/trauma.md).

Epistemic trauma is **not** a license for pathologizing all post-EA experience: many subjects recover spontaneously once the EA environment is removed, especially when [delegitimizace](@/glossary/delegitimizace.md) of replacement support sources has not been comprehensive. Persistent epistemic trauma typically indicates advanced or prolonged EA exposure — see [kognitivní anti-rekurzivita](@/glossary/kognitivni-anti-rekurzivita.md) for the hardened-belief endpoint.
