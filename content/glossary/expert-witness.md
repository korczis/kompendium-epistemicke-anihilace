## Definition

An expert witness is a person qualified by specialized knowledge, skill, experience, training, or education to provide opinion testimony on matters beyond the ordinary understanding of jurors or judges. In common-law jurisdictions, governed by Federal Rule of Evidence 702 (and the *Daubert* trilogy of Supreme Court cases), the expert must (1) possess relevant qualifications, (2) employ reliable principles and methods, (3) apply those methods reliably to the facts, and (4) offer testimony that helps the trier of fact. In Czech procedure (zákon č. 254/2019 Sb. o znalcích), the *znalec* must be entered in the public list of experts and prepare a *znalecký posudek* meeting statutory form requirements.

## Manažerské shrnutí

Expert witness (svědek-znalec, v ČR znalec a znalecký posudek) je centrální postava v právních a regulačních řízeních, kde jsou řešeny technické, vědecké nebo odborné otázky přesahující běžnou znalost soudce nebo poroty. V Prismatic Platform vystupují experti v M&A sporech (oceňování, znalecké posudky účetních podvodů), v kybernetických případech (forenzní analýza, atribuce útoků), v insolvenčních řízeních (vyhodnocení transakčního vzorce) a v due-diligence reportech, kde jsou vyžadována stanoviska s nezávislou autoritou. Klíčový rozdíl oproti svědkovi je, že znalec může vyjádřit *názor* (opinion testimony), ale musí prokázat jak svou kvalifikaci, tak vědeckou rigoróznost metodologie. České právo navíc požaduje formální zápis v seznamu znalců.

## Forensic/Legal Context

The U.S. *Daubert v. Merrell Dow* (1993), *General Electric v. Joiner* (1997), and *Kumho Tire v. Carmichael* (1999) trilogy established the modern admissibility framework. The trial court acts as gatekeeper, evaluating whether the expert's methodology is testable, has been tested, has a known error rate, has been peer-reviewed, and enjoys general acceptance in the relevant scientific community. The older *Frye* standard (general acceptance only) survives in a minority of U.S. states and informally influences several civil-law jurisdictions. UK practice under *R v. Turner* and the Criminal Procedure Rules Part 19 imposes analogous reliability scrutiny. Czech znalci face statutory ethical duties (zákon 254/2019 Sb.) and may be sanctioned for negligent or fraudulent posudky; the recent reform tightened qualification audits and disciplinary procedures.

## Practical Application

In due-diligence engagements, independent experts are commissioned for accounting (forensic accountants for QoE), valuation (FMV opinions), technical (security architecture review), and human-capital (organizational assessment) work streams. Selection criteria should include not only credentials but the expert's litigation track record — published methodology, prior court qualifications, *Daubert* exclusion history. In OSINT and intelligence work, the principle of expert authority informs report structure: conclusions should be attributed to named analysts whose qualifications can be evaluated, methodologies should be documented, and uncertainty should be quantified rather than hidden behind unattributed assertions. Compliance teams should preserve the chain from raw data through expert opinion to facilitate later audit or testimony.
