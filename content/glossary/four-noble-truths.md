## Definition

The **Four Noble Truths** (*cattāri ariyasaccāni*) are the diagnostic-therapeutic core of early Buddhist epistemology, attributed to the Dhammacakkappavattana Sutta. Treated *strictly as a formal epistemic structure* — independent of any religious commitment — they articulate a four-step diagnostic schema with substantial structural relevance to contemporary epistemic-damage taxonomies.

The four truths form a **medical analogy** explicitly noted in classical commentaries:

1. **Dukkha** (unsatisfactoriness, dis-ease) — the diagnostic identification: there *is* a problem; the subject's current state is structurally unsatisfactory. Not "everything is suffering" in the popular caricature, but: the conditioned, reactive mode of cognition is constitutively unstable.
2. **Samudaya** (origin) — the etiology: the unsatisfactoriness has *causes*, specifically craving (*taṇhā*) and ignorance (*avijjā*) regarding the conditioned nature of experience.
3. **Nirodha** (cessation) — the prognosis: the condition is *not* permanent; the causal chain can be interrupted.
4. **Magga** (path) — the therapy: the eightfold practical protocol for interruption.

What makes the structure epistemically interesting — and the reason for inclusion here — is the **diagnostic discipline** it imposes: identify the disease, identify its causes, establish that cessation is possible, specify the protocol. This is structurally identical to the diagnostic-therapeutic logic of the [Kompendium](@/kompendium/_index.md): identify the EA technique (EA-X.Y.Z-α), identify its mechanism, establish that recovery is possible, specify the recovery protocol.

A related structural feature: the third truth (*nirodha*) is non-trivial. It blocks the despair-collapse that would otherwise follow from the first two truths. In epistemic-damage contexts, the parallel is critical — recognizing damage without recognizing the *possibility* of recovery is itself an EA-amplifying state.

## Context

Within the Prismatic Platform, the Four Noble Truths structure is referenced not as religious content but as a **comparative diagnostic framework** in [Kompendium → 06-zotaveni → ramce](/kompendium/06-zotaveni/ramce/), alongside Aristotelian *eudaimonia*, Stoic *prosochē*, and CBT/ACT therapeutic schemas. The cross-traditional comparison serves a specific purpose: identifying the **invariant features** of any credible epistemic-damage recovery framework.

Three invariants emerge across all traditions: (1) **diagnostic specificity** — name the damage precisely; (2) **causal etiology** — identify mechanism, not just symptom; (3) **path specification** — make the recovery protocol enumerable, repeatable, and falsifiable. The platform's {{ cross_link(path="/hub/dd/decisions/", text="Decision Engine") }} recovery-tracking logic enforces all three: a "recovery plan" without enumerated steps fails validation.

The *anatta* / non-self component of Buddhist epistemology is *not* incorporated into the platform — it carries metaphysical commitments orthogonal to the diagnostic question. The platform's use is restricted to the structural skeleton.
