## Definition

**Lebenswelt** — usually translated as **lifeworld** — is Edmund Husserl's term, developed centrally in *The Crisis of European Sciences and Transcendental Phenomenology* (1936), for the **pre-theoretical, taken-for-granted horizon** of immediate experience that grounds all subsequent cognitive activity, including scientific theory. The lifeworld is not what we *think about*; it is the background against which any thinking-about can occur.

Husserl developed the concept against what he diagnosed as the "crisis" of modern science: the substitution of an idealized mathematical-physical universe for the lifeworld in which that science was originally meaningful. The lifeworld is *prior* to and *founding for* every theoretical construction — including the theoretical construction that purports to replace it. We do not first encounter electrons and then construct a lifeworld; we first inhabit a lifeworld in which the activity of *measuring* and *theorizing* is meaningful, and within that activity we develop the concept of an electron.

Four structural features of the lifeworld matter for epistemic security: (1) **givenness** — it is *already there*, not produced by an act of attention; (2) **horizonal structure** — it is not a totality of objects but a structured background of possibilities; (3) **intersubjectivity** — it is shared, not private (a "lifeworld of one" is incoherent); (4) **historicity** — every lifeworld is sedimented from prior generations' active life, embedded in language, institutions, practices.

The lifeworld is the **deepest stratum of cognition**. It is also therefore the deepest target of [Epistemická anihilace](/glossary/epistemicka-anihilace/): damage to specific beliefs is recoverable; damage to the lifeworld threatens the *condition of possibility* for any belief at all.

## Context

Within the Prismatic Platform threat model, lifeworld-damage is treated as the **highest severity stratum** in the [Kompendium taxonomy](/glossary/taxonomie-ea/). The EA-6.x family — the most severe — operates explicitly at this level: techniques that disrupt the subject's basic sense of *what is real, what is shared, what is taken for granted*. Documentation: [Kompendium → 03-mechanismy → lebenswelt](/kompendium/03-mechanismy/lebenswelt/) and [/kompendium/06-zotaveni/lifeworld/](/kompendium/06-zotaveni/lifeworld/).

Three diagnostic markers of lifeworld-damage drive the platform's [Decision Engine](/hub/dd/decisions/) severity calibration: (1) **derealization** — the subject reports that *the world itself* has become unreal or thin; (2) **intersubjective collapse** — the subject can no longer assume shared reality with others, including non-adversarial others; (3) **horizon collapse** — the structured background of possibilities flattens into either claustrophobia or vertiginous undifferentiation.

These markers are *not* symptoms in the standard psychiatric sense — they are structural-phenomenological diagnostics that *precede* and *underlie* psychiatric symptomatology. The platform's recovery doctrine prioritizes lifeworld-restoration over symptom-management: stabilizing intersubjective ground (trusted others, shared everyday practices, repetitive embodied activities) before any propositional therapy. This sequencing is documented at [/kompendium/06-zotaveni/sekvence/](/kompendium/06-zotaveni/sekvence/).
