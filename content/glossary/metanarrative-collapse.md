## Definition

**Metanarrative collapse** is shorthand for the diagnosis Jean-François Lyotard offers in *La condition postmoderne* (*The Postmodern Condition*, 1979): the postmodern condition is defined by **incredulity toward metanarratives** (*incrédulité à l'égard des métarécits*). A metanarrative — also called a grand narrative or *grand récit* — is a totalizing story that legitimizes knowledge-claims and social practices by appeal to an overarching philosophy of history: the Enlightenment narrative of progressive emancipation through reason, the Hegelian narrative of Spirit's self-realization, the Marxist narrative of class struggle culminating in communism, the liberal narrative of free-market convergence, the Christian narrative of providential history.

Lyotard's claim is that under postmodern conditions — late capitalism, computerization of knowledge, professionalization of expertise — these legitimizing stories have lost credibility. What replaces them is not a single new narrative but a **plurality of local language games**, each with its own internal rules, performative criteria, and limited scope. Knowledge no longer organizes itself under a single legitimizing umbrella; it disperses into incommensurable local discourses with no shared meta-criterion to adjudicate between them.

This is not, for Lyotard, primarily a celebration. The collapse of metanarratives leaves emancipatory political projects without their inherited legitimation structures, and creates the conditions under which **performativity** (efficiency, optimization) can fill the legitimacy vacuum — anticipating concerns that overlap with [instrumental reason](@/glossary/instrumental-reason.md) and [power-knowledge](@/glossary/power-knowledge.md).

## Context

The Kompendium uses Lyotard's diagnosis to explain a structural feature of contemporary delegitimization techniques: in an environment where appeal to shared metanarratives is itself suspect, **EA-1.2.x delegitimization moves** become both easier (no shared frame to defend) and harder to identify (no shared frame from which to identify them as illegitimate).

Specifically:

- **EA-1.2.x delegitimization at societal scale** weaponizes metanarrative collapse: the victim's appeal to scientific consensus, journalistic standards, judicial procedure, or historical record can be neutralized by reframing each as "just one narrative among others," creating false equivalence between epistemic infrastructures with very different validity-credentials.
- **Algorithmic information environments** accelerate the collapse: the splintering of public discourse into recommendation-curated sub-publics produces precisely the local-language-game dispersal Lyotard describes — but optimized for engagement rather than for substantive incommensurability.
- The kompendium at [/kompendium/07-teoreticke-reflexe/](@/kompendium/07-teoreticke-reflexe/_index.md) treats Lyotard alongside [simulacra](@/glossary/simulacra.md) and [différance](@/glossary/differance.md) as the **postmodern theoretical pillar**, and pairs the postmodern diagnosis with [Habermasian communicative rationality](@/glossary/communicative-rationality.md) as the normative response: the question is not whether to defend a specific metanarrative, but how to maintain conditions of [legitimate discourse](@/glossary/ideal-speech-situation.md) without one.
- The {{ cross_link(path="/hub/dd/decisions/", text="Decision Engine") }} refuses to operate under any single metanarrative by design: it tracks **explicit calibration metrics** (Brier, ECE) that can be applied across narrative frames, and it surfaces frame-dependence rather than hiding it.
- The kompendium itself, by deliberately producing a **technical taxonomy in Czech** anchored in cross-disciplinary scientific literature, performs a kind of **post-metanarrative legitimation** — it does not appeal to a grand narrative but offers itself for testing within multiple local language games (clinical, philosophical, computational).
