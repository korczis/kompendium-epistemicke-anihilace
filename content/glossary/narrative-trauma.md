## Definition

**Narrative trauma** — developed centrally by Arne Johan Vetlesen (notably in *A Philosophy of Pain*, 2009, and *Evil and Human Agency*, 2005) — is damage to the subject's capacity to integrate lived experience into a coherent self-narrative. The concept builds on Paul Ricoeur's *Time and Narrative* and his thesis of **narrative identity**: selfhood is not a metaphysical substance but a temporally-extended narrative the subject continuously composes, revises, and inhabits.

Three structural features matter. (1) **Selfhood is narrative-constituted** — to be a self is to be the protagonist of a story you can recognize as yours, and that story has temporal depth (past, present, projected future). (2) **Narrative coherence is action-enabling** — agency requires a self-narrative within which action is intelligible; an incoherent self cannot deliberate, choose, or commit. (3) **Narrative coherence is not optional** — humans cannot inhabit narrative incoherence indefinitely; under sustained narrative breakdown, the subject either collapses or accepts an externally-supplied narrative.

Narrative trauma is what occurs when the subject's lived experience cannot be integrated into the existing self-narrative *and* the construction of a revised self-narrative is blocked. The classical sources include atrocity (Améry, Levi), domestic abuse (Herman), war (Shay), and — increasingly recognized — sustained interpersonal manipulation. The damage is not at the level of memory contents but at the level of the *integrative process* that makes contents into a story.

For epistemic security, the operational concern is that **narrative trauma creates a vacuum that an attacker can fill**. A subject in narrative breakdown is uniquely vulnerable to externally-supplied narrative scaffolding, regardless of whether the supplied narrative is accurate. This is the structural mechanism behind several long-form EA techniques and explains why post-EA recovery is often a *narrative* problem more than a *cognitive* one.

## Context

Within the Prismatic Platform threat model, narrative trauma is recognized as both a **target** of certain EA techniques and a **substrate** that amplifies the impact of others. Techniques that produce narrative trauma directly are catalogued under EA-4.x in the [Kompendium taxonomy](@/glossary/taxonomie-ea.md) — sustained role inversion, retroactive reinterpretation of shared history, deliberate temporal disorientation. Documentation: [Kompendium → 04-techniky → narrativni-trauma](/kompendium/04-techniky/narrativni-trauma/).

Recovery doctrine treats narrative reconstruction as a **structural prerequisite** for any deeper epistemic recovery: damaged calibration cannot be repaired in a subject without a stable narrative platform to repair it from. The recovery sequence — documented at [/kompendium/06-zotaveni/narativ/](/kompendium/06-zotaveni/narativ/) — proceeds: (1) stabilize embodied present; (2) reconstruct *minimal* coherent narrative from verifiable anchors; (3) integrate trauma-content into the narrative without collapsing it; (4) restore projected-future agency.

The platform's {{ cross_link(path="/hub/dd/decisions/", text="Decision Engine") }} treats reports of narrative discontinuity (subject cannot give a coherent account of recent months; subject's account of the same period changes substantially across sessions; subject describes their past life as belonging to "a different person") as a high-severity calibration alert. This is connected to the [Epistemicke trauma](@/glossary/epistemicke-trauma.md) concept and feeds into the recovery-tracking workflow.
