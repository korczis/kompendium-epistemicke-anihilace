## Definition

The structural-sociological theory of **propaganda** developed by Jacques Ellul in *Propagandes* (1962, English: *Propaganda: The Formation of Men's Attitudes*, 1965) is — within the platform's threat model — the canonical reference. Ellul's central thesis: propaganda is not a series of discrete persuasive messages but a **total sociological phenomenon** operating at population scale through the structural saturation of the modern media environment. The relevant mechanism is not individual deception but **environmental conditioning**: the subject's entire information ecology is restructured.

Ellul's distinctions remain operationally definitive:

- **Agitation propaganda** vs **integration propaganda**: the former mobilizes for short-term action (revolution, war, mobilization); the latter integrates the subject into permanent stable conformity with existing power structures.
- **Vertical** vs **horizontal**: vertical propaganda flows from leader to mass; horizontal propaganda is generated *within* peer groups under structural conditions that make conforming opinion-formation feel organic.
- **Sociological** vs **political**: sociological propaganda is the diffuse, ambient, unintentional propagation of a way of life; political propaganda is targeted and intentional.

Three structural features distinguish Ellulian propaganda from earlier theories: (1) **necessity** — modern populations *require* propaganda to function as integrated political subjects; the demand is structural, not artificial; (2) **totality** — effective propaganda requires total media saturation; partial saturation merely creates resistance; (3) **technique** — propaganda is a *technical* phenomenon governed by laws of effectiveness, not by the moral intentions of any individual propagandist.

For epistemic security, Ellul's framework provides the **scale-up vocabulary**: the same structural phenomena that, at individual scale, manifest as [Epistemická anihilace](@/glossary/epistemicka-anihilace.md) techniques manifest at population scale as propaganda systems. The relationship is not metaphorical — it is structurally homologous: an EA technique successfully deployed across a population *is* a propaganda technique.

## Context

Within the Prismatic Platform's threat model, Ellulian propaganda is the **population-scale analogue** of EA. The [Kompendium taxonomy](@/glossary/taxonomie-ea.md) reserves the EA-7.x family for population-level techniques, with explicit cross-references to Ellul's analysis. Documentation: [Kompendium → 07-populacni → propaganda](@/kompendium/07-populacni/propaganda.md).

Operationally, the platform's {{ <cross_link path="/osint/toolbox" text="OSINT" /> }} infrastructure — particularly the 47 Czech and 80 global adapters — is configured to detect **structural signatures** of integration propaganda in target information environments: media-source homogenization, opinion-piece convergence patterns, vocabulary substitution rates, horizontal-pseudo-organic content amplification. These signals feed into the {{ <cross_link path="/hub/dd/decisions/" text="Decision Engine" /> }} as environmental-context features when assessing individual-level claims.

The structural distinction between agitation propaganda (recognizable, polemical, mobilizing) and integration propaganda (ambient, naturalized, demobilizing) is operationally critical: integration propaganda is harder to detect because it does not look like propaganda — it looks like *the way things are*. The platform's detection logic explicitly tunes for the integration variety, since agitation propaganda is comparatively easier to identify by anyone, including the subject. This connects directly to the [Lebenswelt](@/glossary/lebenswelt.md) concept: integration propaganda operates by reshaping the lifeworld itself.
