## Definition

**Simulacra** is Jean Baudrillard's central concept, developed in *Symbolic Exchange and Death* (1976) and *Simulacra and Simulation* (1981). Baudrillard distinguishes **three orders** of the image's relation to reality, marking a historical trajectory:

1. **First order — counterfeit (Renaissance to Industrial Revolution).** The image is a **reflection** of a profound reality. Representation has a referent; the sign points to something stable and prior.
2. **Second order — production (Industrial era).** The image **masks and denatures** a profound reality. Mass reproduction (photography, lithography, factory production) introduces equivalence; the original loses its aura but the relation to reality is still preserved as the masked term.
3. **Third order — simulation (post-war / mediatic era).** The image **masks the absence** of a profound reality. There is no longer an original; signs refer only to other signs, generating **hyperreality** — a system of signs more real than any reality, because reality itself has receded behind the operational autonomy of the sign-system.
4. **Fourth order — pure simulacrum.** The image **bears no relation to any reality whatsoever**. The simulacrum is its own pure self-reference; the question of correspondence is no longer meaningful.

Baudrillard's claim is not that lies have multiplied — lies still presuppose truth. The simulacrum has passed beyond the true / false distinction into a regime where **the difference itself has been dissolved**.

## Context

The Kompendium uses Baudrillard's analysis to make legible the most extreme reaches of epistemic annihilation: cases in which the victim is no longer being lied to but is being relocated into a hyperreal environment where the lie / truth distinction itself has been dismantled. This is the structural template for **ontological annihilation** — destruction not of specific beliefs but of the very conditions under which beliefs could correspond or fail to correspond to reality.

Specifically:

- **Advanced EA-2.x metakognitivní destrukce** progresses through Baudrillard's orders: early-stage manipulation operates in the second order (lies that mask truth); mature manipulation operates in the third (lies that mask the absence of accessible truth); terminal-stage epistemic annihilation generates a fourth-order environment in which the victim has no leverage to even pose the truth-question. See [/glossary/metakognitivni-destrukce/](@/glossary/metakognitivni-destrukce.md).
- **Mass-scale algorithmic information environments** (deepfakes, generative AI, recommendation-curated reality) are the contemporary infrastructure of third- and fourth-order simulacra — Baudrillard's diagnosis is no longer speculative but operational.
- The kompendium at [/kompendium/07-teoreticke-reflexe/](@/kompendium/07-teoreticke-reflexe/_index.md) treats Baudrillard alongside Lyotard's [metanarrative collapse](@/glossary/metanarrative-collapse.md) and Derridean [différance](@/glossary/differance.md) as the postmodern theoretical pillar — the diagnostic apparatus for **late-stage** EA environments where modern (correspondence-theoretic) defenses are insufficient.
- The [Decision Engine](/hub/dd/decisions/) explicit **provenance tracking** and **source diversity auditing** are anti-simulacral architectural commitments: they refuse to operate purely on internal coherence and demand grounding in extra-systemic reality checks.
- The kompendium's clinical applications at [/kompendium/06-klinicke-aplikace/](@/kompendium/06-klinicke-aplikace/_index.md) describe interventions specifically designed to re-anchor patients who have spent extended periods in third- or fourth-order simulacral environments.
