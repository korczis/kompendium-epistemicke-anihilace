## Definition

Trauma bonding describes the strong emotional attachment that forms when an abused person becomes psychologically tied to the abuser through a repeating cycle of threat, harm, and reconciliation interspersed with periods of warmth, validation, or relief. The term was popularized by psychologist Patrick Carnes and is grounded in operant-conditioning research on intermittent reinforcement, which produces the most resistant behavioral responses. Trauma bonds explain why victims of domestic abuse, hostage-takers, traffickers, cults, and high-control employers frequently defend, return to, or remain loyal to those who harm them.

## Manažerské shrnutí

Trauma bonding není slabost ani špatný úsudek oběti — je to neurologicky podložený mechanismus přežití, ve kterém střídání hrozby a úlevy aktivuje stejné systémy odměn jako závislostní chování. V investigativní a DD praxi je rozpoznání trauma bondu klíčové pro správné vedení rozhovorů (svědek může chránit pachatele, popírat zjevné fakty, projevovat racionalizační vzorce) i pro hodnocení rozhodovací kapacity protistran v transakcích, kde existuje podezření na nepatřičný vliv. Nesprávná interpretace projevů trauma bondu jako "souhlasu" nebo "spolupráce" je častou chybou méně zkušených vyšetřovatelů.

## Forensic/Legal Context

In criminal proceedings, trauma bonding is regularly invoked to explain delayed disclosure, victim recantation, and "uncooperative" witnesses in domestic violence and human-trafficking cases. Courts in multiple jurisdictions have admitted expert testimony on trauma bonding to rebut the inference that a victim's continued contact with an abuser implies consent or fabrication. In civil contexts — undue-influence challenges to wills, contracts signed under coercion, employment termination of cult members — trauma bonding evidence supports claims that ostensibly voluntary acts were not the product of free will. The diagnostic literature distinguishes trauma bonding from genuine attachment, dependency disorders, and Stockholm syndrome (a narrower phenomenon specific to captive-captor relationships).

## Practical Application

Investigators conducting forensic interviews with suspected trauma-bonded subjects must use trauma-informed protocols: avoid leading questions that imply victim culpability, allow non-linear narrative recall, and recognize that idealization of the abuser is a defense mechanism, not an exculpatory statement. In due-diligence vetting of high-net-worth principals or politically exposed persons, trauma-bonded relationships within the household can indicate hidden coercion that affects beneficial-ownership determinations and AML risk. Therapy modalities with empirical support include trauma-focused CBT, EMDR, and Internal Family Systems; recovery typically requires physical separation plus structured psychoeducation about the bonding mechanism itself.
